# Nosh can make unfamiliar food approachable

November 1, 2026 · Research and strategy proposal; not an accepted product change

**Dish-first Nosh is a promising discovery hypothesis, but it is not a shortcut around restaurant-data maintenance.** A food ontology could help Nosh introduce unfamiliar dishes through qualities someone already enjoys, and that knowledge can travel across cities. However, delivering the dish requires an additional, changing layer of restaurant menus, specific variants, availability and ordering. Nosh’s accepted architecture already avoids maintaining a comprehensive restaurant catalog by using live Google Places. The strongest reason to explore this direction is therefore a better consumer experience and a more reusable understanding of taste, rather than lower total operating work. Start with a small collection of obtainable dishes and test whether the recommendations produce enjoyable meals. Neither “all the foods in the world” nor automatic delivery needs to precede that test. ([Nosh decisions](/Users/winstontang/Downloads/nosh/DECISIONS.md))

## Dish knowledge travels; available meals remain local

The idea combines three products. **Culinary discovery** suggests a dish and remembers interest; users then work out how to eat it. **Local dish discovery** connects the suggestion to a particular restaurant offering. **Automatic delivery** also arranges the purchase and fulfillment. The first can avoid restaurant inventory entirely, but leaves the user with more work. The second preserves Nosh’s practical meal-time purpose while adding menu-resolution work. The third offers the experience shown in the reel, with substantially greater commercial and operational dependencies. These are strategic assessments, not measured cost estimates.

This distinction changes the comparison with existing Nosh. Decisions D021, D022 and D027 already replace a curated restaurant database with live Google Places and retained provider IDs plus Nosh-owned events. A dish graph would add a durable semantic asset to that plan; it would not remove an owned restaurant catalog the plan still requires. It could reduce duplicated culinary classification across cities, but finding actual servings would remain local. ([Nosh decisions](/Users/winstontang/Downloads/nosh/DECISIONS.md))

Google Places can help identify candidates. Text Search supports food-oriented queries, but returns places. Its documented restaurant fields include opening hours, price information and whether delivery is supported, rather than a structured catalog of menu-item identifiers, ingredients, modifiers, item stock and checkout. Searching for a dish nearby is therefore useful evidence to investigate, not proof that a particular serving can be bought now. ([Text Search](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/text-search), [Place schema](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/reference/rest/v1/places))

A usable model should keep four things separate: the **dish concept**, a **recipe or regional variant**, a restaurant’s **specific menu offering**, and its **availability for this meal**. “Dumpling” is a concept; a particular filling and preparation is a variant; a restaurant’s six-piece item is an offering; availability depends on its current service and fulfillment conditions. Knowing the restaurant is open does not establish that the relevant menu is active, the item remains available, or delivery reaches the customer. Uber’s menu model explicitly distinguishes menus, service hours, items and modifiers, illustrating this additional operational detail. ([Uber menu integration](https://developer.uber.com/docs/eats/guides/menu-integration))

**The sustainability question is whether better discovery earns the additional work.** Measure founder time spent checking offerings, correcting dish matches and resolving failed recommendations alongside provider bills. No reviewed evidence supports a percentage saving or demonstrates that commercial menu coverage fits Nosh’s approximately $50 monthly infrastructure budget.

## Build a useful food vocabulary before a global ontology

The underlying technology is feasible, and Nosh does not need to invent its entire vocabulary. FoodOn supplies food-product, material and process concepts under CC BY 4.0. Wikidata’s structured data is CC0. USDA FoodData Central offers public-domain food and nutrient information. These are useful building blocks, but none establishes comprehensive coverage of restaurant dishes, sensory characteristics and current offerings. FoodOn explicitly emphasizes vocabulary and acknowledges that product descriptions vary in detail; USDA’s nutrient-oriented records serve a different purpose from live menus. ([FoodOn](https://foodon.org/), [FoodOn license](https://github.com/FoodOntology/foodon/blob/master/LICENSE.txt), [Wikidata licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing), [USDA API guide](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/))

Nosh’s contribution would be a carefully chosen consumer layer: names and aliases, dish families, typical ingredients, preparation, texture, flavor intensity and cultural associations. Represent regional relationships and variants rather than forcing every dish into one country’s branch. Preserve uncertainty: “typically contains” differs from “always contains.” A useful graph connects concepts with understandable relationships; it does not need to resolve every disputed origin or recipe variation before recommending lunch.

Data licensing needs to be part of this design. FlavorDB’s FAQ specifies a noncommercial share-alike license, so it is not an automatically suitable commercial data feed. FoodKG demonstrates how recipes, nutrients and FoodOn can be connected, but its construction requires separately obtaining Recipe1M. This review did not establish Recipe1M’s commercial dataset rights. Open code, linked papers and downloadable data do not necessarily grant the same permissions. Track the source, license, version and confidence of imported facts, and assess images and prose separately. ([FlavorDB FAQ](https://cosylab.iiitd.edu.in/flavordb/faq), [FoodKG construction](https://foodkg.github.io/foodkg.html))

Google-derived content needs a separate boundary. Places permits retention of place IDs while restricting other caching, and its terms restrict creating content from Maps content and specified AI uses. Extracting tags from Google reviews should not be assumed to create an independently owned ontology. Durable dish descriptions and merchant mappings need appropriate independent sources or permissions. ([Places policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies), [Google Maps Platform terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms))

A modest graph can run in Nosh’s existing PostgreSQL database using ordinary records and relationship tables. **A graph-shaped model does not require a graph database.** Begin with understandable attributes and reviewed relationships; introduce embeddings only if evaluation shows that they solve a retrieval problem those simpler tools cannot. This fits the existing decision to defer vector infrastructure until there is a defined use case and evaluation set. ([Architecture](/Users/winstontang/Downloads/nosh/ARCHITECTURE.md), [D013](/Users/winstontang/Downloads/nosh/DECISIONS.md))

## Similarity creates possibilities; meals establish preference

Ontology, semantic search and recommendation perform different jobs. The ontology defines relationships. Semantic search retrieves related concepts or interprets a craving. The recommender chooses what to offer this person now, considering taste, novelty and practical constraints. Fulfillment finds an obtainable instance. Improving one layer does not establish that the others work.

An illustrative bridge might suggest a pan-fried mandu variant to someone who enjoys pan-fried gyoza, explaining the familiar crisp base and savory filling while checking the actual filling and preparation. That is a reason to explore, not a validated prediction about this person. A different texture, seasoning or ingredient can matter more than the shared category. Flavor-network research itself finds different ingredient-compound sharing patterns across regional cuisines; it studies recipe composition, not proof that ingredient similarity predicts individual enjoyment. ([Ahn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.6074))

**Saving the yes is useful, but it records interest rather than satisfaction.** A tap can reflect a photograph, hunger, familiarity, curiosity or an assumed price. Keep the sequence explicit: shown, interested, selected, obtained, eaten and enjoyed. Ask a lightweight post-meal question, such as whether the person would eat it again. Preserve “Not today” as timing feedback. A disappointing serving should not automatically become a dislike of the entire dish, ingredient or cuisine. Nosh already separates acceptance from confirmed experience; that discipline transfers directly to dishes. Research on implicit feedback also cautions that recorded interactions are biased by what the system exposes. ([Nosh feedback semantics](/Users/winstontang/Downloads/nosh/RECOMMENDATIONS.md), [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/attribute-based-propensity-for-unbiased-learning-in-recommender-systems-algorithm-and-case-studies/))

Diet alignment must attach to the correct level of evidence. A generic recipe cannot establish a vendor’s actual substitutions or preparation. Model compatible, incompatible and unknown states; do not treat missing ingredient information as confirmation. This is a limit on what Nosh’s data can claim, rather than an argument against useful dietary filtering.

The competitive evidence also narrows the defensibility claim. DoorDash has described consumer, merchant and item profiles incorporating ingredients, taste and dietary classifications, alongside embeddings and explanations. Its June 2026 Ask DoorDash announcement describes natural-language restaurant search and tailored carts, with an initially limited rollout. These are first-party capability claims, not independent evidence of effectiveness, but they show that semantic food discovery is already an active competitive area. ([DoorDash engineering](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/doordash-profile-generation-llms-understanding-consumers-merchants-and-items/), [Ask DoorDash](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/ask-doordash))

Nosh’s stronger potential asset is evidence about **which unfamiliar foods become favorites for particular people**, paired with reliable opportunities to try them. That asset would emerge from repeated useful service. An ontology can be central to the architecture, while the product promise remains “discover your next favorite dish.” Selling the ontology or search API to other businesses would be a separate proposition requiring evidence of buyers and willingness to pay.

## Test enjoyable discovery before promising delivery

The reel’s seamless delivery is not simply an extra API call. DoorDash Marketplace access is limited and primarily concerns partner stores, menus and orders placed on DoorDash. Uber Eats integrations require partner approval and merchant authorization. Courier products such as Uber Direct dispatch delivery for an existing order; they do not independently arrange arbitrary restaurants’ preparation and purchase. DoorDash Drive also documents restricted production access. Nosh’s eligibility, contract terms and ordering access remain unknown. ([DoorDash Marketplace](https://developer.doordash.com/en-US/docs/marketplace/overview/), [Uber Eats onboarding](https://developer.uber.com/docs/eats/guides/getting-started), [Uber authorization](https://developer.uber.com/docs/eats/guides/authentication), [Uber Direct](https://developer.uber.com/docs/deliveries/overview), [DoorDash Drive](https://developer.doordash.com/en-US/docs/drive/tutorials/get_started/))

A discovery pilot can instead show the actual offering and hand the person to a verified restaurant or ordering page for review and checkout. Direct relationships with a few merchants could support a later controlled delivery experiment, but introduce onboarding and operational work. Separate these tests so delivery convenience does not masquerade as evidence that the ontology recommends better food.

An illustrative first trial would use **20–50 locally obtainable dish concepts, 10–15 consenting users and two to three weeks**. These are practical scope limits, not statistically powered targets. Collect a few dishes each person already enjoys and their explicit constraints. First determine whether appealing suggestions can be matched to current, affordable offerings without excessive manual checking. Then compare graph-informed suggestions with simple curated neighbors or tags, using comparable supply and presentation. A separate restaurant-first comparison can test the framing; changing framing and ranking together would obscure what caused any improvement.

Track confirmed eating, enjoyment, willingness to eat again, voluntary repeat use, unavailable suggestions, decision effort, notification fatigue and maintenance minutes per completed meal. Set acceptable thresholds before beginning. Continue if people repeatedly enjoy unfamiliar dishes and the work fits the founder’s budget. Simplify if tags perform as well as the graph; reconsider local fulfillment if availability defeats the experience. Enthusiastic reactions to the concept alone are insufficient evidence.

Most of the existing foundation remains useful: authentication, schedules, user ownership, reliable writes, sessions and outcome confirmation. A chosen pivot would require explicit decisions about dish identity, repeat restaurants, source rights, notification content and success metrics. “Confirmed positively rated new dishes” becomes a plausible outcome because a new dish at a familiar restaurant can succeed. The current location-free notification design also means dish-specific local promises need deliberate handling. These are proposed changes, not updates made by this research. ([Implementation handoff](/Users/winstontang/Downloads/nosh/IMPLEMENTATION_HANDOFF.md), [Product requirements](/Users/winstontang/Downloads/nosh/PRODUCT.md))

The next investment should establish whether Nosh helps people cross a culinary boundary they would otherwise avoid, with little effort and a good meal afterward. If it does, the ontology can grow from demonstrated discoveries and actual demand. That would give the knowledge structure a practical purpose and a sustainable expansion rule, without making worldwide completeness the prerequisite for delivering value.
