# Consumer pivots and incumbent substitution

Research date: November 1, 2026. US/San Francisco context. These notes reconsider the problem and business, not just how to defend the current implementation. No pivot is accepted, no implementation is authorized, and prior Nosh strategy reports are proposals. Public feature descriptions establish product capabilities or positioning; they do not establish unmet demand, retention, profitability, satisfaction, or Nosh's ability to acquire customers. Prices are advertised offers observed on the research date, not evidence of realized willingness to pay or revenue.

User clarification during research: prioritize a company with substantial scale potential and/or a useful consumer product that can grow gradually, rather than optimizing for a small founder-operated service business. The scalability addendum below supersedes any ordering inferred from the initial conditional shortlist. Concierge work is principally a validation method unless there is evidence it can turn into a repeatable product.

## What jobs are already served by incumbents and ordinary substitutes?

### Takeaway
Personalized discovery, saved lists, social taste, proximity reminders, shared planning, proactive local suggestions and creator monetization all have existing products. There may still be underserved people, but a feature diagram cannot identify them. The likely useful question is which specific person repeatedly fails to get an outcome despite these tools, and what they already spend to solve it.

### Cited Findings
- **DoorDash is not confined to delivery.** Its September 30, 2025 Going Out announcement combines restaurant discovery, in-store rewards and reservations, with reservation rollout initially described for New York and Miami. Do not infer universal geographic availability or current SF reservation coverage from the announcement. It demonstrates that in-person dining is already within its strategic scope. [DoorDash Going Out](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-launches-going-out)
- **Google combines personalized discovery and action.** The March 12, 2026 Ask Maps announcement describes conversational questions, personalization from searched/saved places, and a concrete example of finding a convenient midway restaurant for four friends with vegan options. It describes reservation, save, share and directions actions; initial rollout was US/India Android/iOS. This defeats the assumption that only a specialist startup can understand occasion and group constraints. It does not prove every recommendation is accurate. [Google Ask Maps](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/)
- **Google already owns a saved-intent workflow.** Maps supports lists of favorite or wanted places, notes, sharing and inviting others to edit. [Google Maps list help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/7280933?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&hl=en)
- **Yelp operates below the restaurant-category level.** Its October 21, 2025 release describes natural-language/voice search, restaurant business-page questions grounded in reviews/photos/business information, and Menu Vision surfacing dish photos/reviews from a scanned menu. The release includes rollout language; it is not a comprehensive field test. [Yelp fall release](https://www.yelp-ir.com/news/press-releases/news-release-details/2025/Yelp-Expands-AI-Features-to-Make-Local-Discovery-More-Conversational-Visual-and-Seamless/default.aspx)
- **Beli covers taste and social discovery.** The developer's US App Store listing advertises ranked visited/want-to-try lists/maps, friends' dining, personalized recommendations, notes/favorite dishes, Taste Profiles and friend Match Scores. It is free with purchases. Observed listed SKUs include Beli Supper Club annual at $74.99 and creator subscriptions at $5.79 monthly / $42.90 annual. SKU labels/prices do not establish exact entitlements, all users' checkout offers or subscriber counts. [Beli US listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/beli/id1478375386)
- **Mapstr already activates saved intent.** Its FAQ describes proximity alerts for wanted places, visit logging, collaborative maps and creator profiles. It allows up to 20 collaborators free, according to the FAQ. Plus prices are given as examples of €59/year or €7.90/month in Europe, explicitly varying by country; these are not US prices. It also describes available-table filtering and booking. “Remind me near a save” is therefore not an unoccupied wedge. [Mapstr FAQ](https://en.mapstr.com/faq)
- **Instagram already combines saves with the existing social graph.** Meta's March 29, 2023 Japanese-language announcement describes collaborative saved collections with friends/groups, adding from feed/Reels or DMs and notifying the shared conversation. This is an official announcement translated here, not an API integration guarantee. [Meta collaborative collections](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2023/03/collaborativecollection/)
- **TikTok competes for local discovery attention.** The February 11, 2026 US Local Feed announcement covers nearby restaurants, shops, travel and events from creators/businesses, ranked with location, topic and freshness signals. [TikTok Local Feed](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-the-local-feed?lang=en)
- **Cobble explicitly promises agreement and action.** Its official site presents finding activity ideas, sharing a poll and going together, with restaurant examples and local guides. It directly targets the “what do you want to do?” exchange. Do not confuse it with unrelated products named Cobble or assume nationwide coverage. [Cobble](https://www.trycobble.com/)
- **Partiful turns availability into commitment.** Its June 16, 2026 help page describes Yes/No/Maybe date polls; selecting a time notifies guests and converts responses into RSVPs. A separate fee page says free tickets incur no fees; paid ticket fees depend on event size and price. The worked $112 ticket/$100 payout example must not be generalized into a universal 12% tariff. [Partiful time polling](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525423-using-find-a-time-to-poll-guests-on-date-and-time), [fees](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525398-what-fees-does-partiful-charge)
- **Howbout offers free shared availability.** Official support says the iOS/Android shared calendar is free and can show friends' availability, add plans and sync Google/Apple calendars. [Howbout](https://howbout.app/get-help/is-there-a-free-shared-calendar-app-for-friends/)
- **Doodle offers a free scheduling substitute.** The current pricing page lists a $0 plan with one Group Poll, Booking Page and 1:1. Paid tiers add unlimited polls and advanced deadlines/reminders. Paid dollar values were not consistently rendered; do not invent them. [Doodle pricing](https://doodle.com/en/premium)
- **Timeleft sells an organized social outcome.** Its current FAQ describes matching small groups, booking venues and showing attendees where to go. Membership covers organization; guests pay venues separately. Membership price varies by country/plan and is shown in the app; no reliable fixed US price was established here. It supports repeated connections and multiple formats, so do not characterize it as only one-off stranger dinners. [Timeleft FAQ](https://timeleft.com/faq/)
- **Proactive curated plans already have a paid offer.** The Nudge says it researches activities, builds plans and texts them to users. Its Plus page advertises a two-week trial followed by $69.99/year, displayed as $5.79/month, and features including Ask Nudge, a map and Travel Mode. Preserve the site's annual amount rather than deriving an exact monthly equivalent from its rounded display. [Nudge](https://www.nudgetext.com/), [Plus offer](https://www.nudgetext.com/plus)
- **Creator guide infrastructure exists.** Thatch's seller documentation describes tips, paywalls and services, with creators keeping 90% and a 10% platform fee. Its main site was not accessible to this browser, so the assessment relies on this official seller documentation, not a live catalog audit. [Thatch seller documentation](https://thatch.notion.site/Become-a-Seller-bd37d343266141b78203137f25424852)
- **Local experiential competitors sell more than recommendations.** Sidewalk Food Tours' SF page offers neighborhood food tours combining tastings and local guides, including private/corporate formats. No fixed ticket price was reliably captured. [Sidewalk SF](https://sidewalkfoodtours.com/san-francisco/)
- **Workplace introductions are also occupied.** Donut's pricing page offers free introductions for up to 24 users per round in one Slack channel, with paid plans supporting expanded introductions and customization. Its standard price renders both $74/$89 monthly figures for different billing views; avoid quoting one without its billing term. [Donut pricing](https://www.donut.com/pricing/)

### Inferences
- The competition is a workflow assembled from tools: an Instagram save, a Google Maps list, a group text, a calendar invitation and a restaurant call can already complete the job. An app must reduce meaningful work or produce an outcome these alternatives repeatedly fail to produce; adding another place to maintain data can make the problem worse.
- Do not assert incumbents lack one-pick, proactive, dish, semantic, novelty or cross-cuisine recommendations. The reviewed evidence positively establishes many adjacent capabilities but is not exhaustive enough to support absence claims.
- A feature can be valuable without being a standalone business. A specialized promise may improve conversion for a tiny group while still failing retention, distribution or willingness-to-pay tests.
- **Avoid incumbent fatalism.** A competitor having a feature does not establish that every user's problem is solved, that its execution is good, that it reaches every segment, or that the market is saturated. It raises the required test: show why a particular group still fails despite a readily available substitute. Equally, a fresh interface, one-pick display or notification is not by itself durable differentiation.
- The founder's demonstrated asset is willingness to create Nosh reels and build software. The record does not establish a large audience, exclusive restaurant access, office/community distribution, culinary authority or paying users. Each proposed channel below is something to test, not an asset assumed to exist.

### Gaps
- No representative primary customer-interview evidence was found that isolates a large underserved segment for any proposed Nosh consumer pivot.
- Public product claims and listed prices do not establish active paying cohorts or competitors' unit economics.
- Reliable import/access rights to Instagram/TikTok saves were not established. A pilot can use voluntarily submitted links; a production plan cannot assume bulk private-save API access.

## Which consumer hypotheses deserve experiments, and which should be rejected?

### Takeaway
The most defensible experiments are outcome services within a narrow reachable group, not a wider recommendation engine. The following are product hypotheses and proposed tests, not researched facts. Numerical thresholds are explicit founder decision rules for small pilots, not statistical benchmarks. A successful tiny pilot justifies another test, not a claim of product-market fit.

### Cited Findings
The alternatives below are constrained by existing saved/social tools ([Beli](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/beli/id1478375386), [Mapstr](https://en.mapstr.com/faq)), coordination tools ([Cobble](https://www.trycobble.com/), [Partiful](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525423-using-find-a-time-to-poll-guests-on-date-and-time), [Howbout](https://howbout.app/get-help/is-there-a-free-shared-calendar-app-for-friends/)), organized experiences ([Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/faq/), [Sidewalk](https://sidewalkfoodtours.com/san-francisco/)), and paid curation offers ([Nudge Plus](https://www.nudgetext.com/plus), [Thatch](https://thatch.notion.site/Become-a-Seller-bd37d343266141b78203137f25424852)). None of these sources validates Nosh demand.

### Inferences

#### 1. Activate a small saved-intent shortlist

**User/pain:** Someone who can name three restaurants they genuinely intend to visit soon, yet repeatedly fails to choose a time or act. First determine whether the blocker is forgetting, money, distance, company, reservations or merely aspirational saving. Only some are addressable by activation.

**Offer/switch:** Convert three submitted links into one feasible proposal with a concrete time and acceptance deadline. Fit within the user's existing message channel; do not demand full migration or a daily app habit. Switch only if it measurably saves planning effort and causes wanted meals to happen.

**Substitutes:** Maps/Beli/Mapstr lists and alerts, an existing reminder, calendar commitment, or a friend saying “Friday?” The proposed feature is easy to copy. A reliable personal planning relationship could accumulate useful history, but that is not a structural moat yet.

**Buyer/channel:** Consumer subscription or per-plan service is a hypothesis with weak evidence. Recruit through actual reel viewers who respond, and founder contacts who demonstrate a real unvisited shortlist. Neither audience scale nor willingness to pay is established.

**Data/operations:** Low global data requirement, but each proposal needs current hours, budget and feasibility checks. No citywide participant density requirement for solo use. Manual service may consume more time than the consumer values.

**Test/kill:** Invite 10 qualified people to submit three realistic saves and one actual available meal slot. Deliver two proposals each across two weeks. Beforehand set a continuation rule such as five completed wanted meals across at least four people and three people independently requesting another plan. Kill/reshape if the main blocker is budget or no real desire, if founder chasing creates all activity, or if participants prefer a simple recurring reminder. Subsequently ask for a real paid continuation offer; free completion alone is insufficient for a business.

#### 2. Own the recurring private-group planning chore

**User/pain:** The person who repeatedly organizes dinner for the same couple/friend group and resents collecting constraints and chasing decisions. This is an organizer problem before it is a taste-prediction problem.

**Offer/switch:** “Your next dinner is settled by Wednesday”: collect a few constraints, provide one viable choice and a fallback, get explicit responses and finish the plan. Guests should answer a link/message without needing a new social network. An outcome guarantee about planning effort is more meaningful than another shared wishlist.

**Substitutes:** Group text, Partiful/Doodle, Howbout, Cobble, rotating who picks and simply returning to a favorite restaurant. Existing tools already solve parts of the workflow.

**Buyer/channel:** Organizer pays for saved labor, or participants split an explicitly disclosed planning fee. Start with five real groups reachable through founders' actual contacts; do not assume access to a community. A family/couple subscription remains unproven.

**Data/operations/advantage:** No stranger-network cold start inside an existing group. Still vulnerable to nonresponses, mismatched spending limits and cancellation. Repeated group constraints and trust can reduce future work, but competitors can implement similar features. A human concierge can outperform an app yet fail on labor margins.

**Test/kill:** Manually plan two consecutive meals for five established groups. Track organizer minutes, response chasing, attendance, satisfaction and renewal. Continue only if several organizers both save material time and pay for the next cycle. Reject if the bottleneck is unwillingness to meet rather than logistics, if one habitual organizer still does the work, or if a simple poll performs equivalently. Do not build calendar integrations before proving value with explicit availability.

#### 3. A closed-community lunch ritual

**User/pain:** New members of one coworking space, campus cohort or office want familiar faces and a dependable low-effort lunch. Community managers might want participation without running logistics themselves.

**Offer/switch:** One fixed weekly lunch slot, one nearby venue, explicit opt-in and small stable/rotating groups. This deliberately concentrates participants rather than requiring an always-on map of hungry strangers.

**Substitutes:** Office Slack, lunch tables, clubs, Donut, existing community hosts and catered events. The value must be actual attendance and useful connections, not introductions sent.

**Buyer/channel:** Community organizer or employer could pay a pilot/program fee; individuals buying lunch does not prove organizer willingness to pay. Contact one organizer the founder can realistically reach. A paid design partner or permission to recruit is a gate, not assumed access. Procurement and building access can make this less lightweight than it sounds.

**Data/operations/advantage:** A small venue pool lowers restaurant data scope. Attendance density is still required within a particular place/time. Food payment remains at the venue. Manual no-show follow-up, group comfort and venue capacity are real work. A durable organizer relationship and embedded ritual are modest distribution advantages; there is no network moat before repeated participation.

**Test/kill:** Secure one community host and run four lunches with roughly 12–20 consenting people. Measure repeat attendance without founder chasing, host time saved and willingness to pay for month two. Stop if no organizer will provide access, if people already organize without help, or if venue/coordination labor exceeds the proposed fee. Do not generalize one enthusiastic office to consumer demand.

#### 4. A creator-led food club or narrow experience

**User/pain:** Followers trust a particular local creator and want a concrete outing, tasting route or guided introduction, rather than another saved reel. The creator may want to earn from local expertise without repetitive DM planning.

**Offer/switch:** A clearly themed paid experience or tiny recurring club: for example a guided introduction to a neighborhood's dishes, with named stops, what to order and a real host. Differentiate through host expertise, access, community or accountability. A static paywalled map alone is directly exposed to Thatch, Mapstr and Beli creator products.

**Buyer/channel:** Attendees pay for an event/guide; alternatively a creator pays for operations only after revenue and labor savings are demonstrated. Use the founder's real reels to solicit reservations, or recruit one creator willing to promote a test. A partnership is an acquisition task; it is not free distribution. Restaurant sponsorship can compromise recommendation trust and should not be assumed as initial revenue.

**Data/operations/advantage:** Bounded, human-verified venues; scheduling, weather, host reliability, cancellations and customer support dominate technology. Tickets are not profit: subtract food, venue, host time, payment fees, refunds and acquisition. Expertise, creator audience and unique access may be harder to copy than software, but the founder has not yet established them. Avoid creating a two-sided creator marketplace first.

**Test/kill:** Publish one real small outing or one specific paid guide with a clear price and fulfillment date; collect actual paid reservations through existing tools before app work. Proposed gate: 6–10 purchases from people beyond close friends, satisfactory fulfillment and positive contribution after valuing host time. Kill if only discounted/free seats fill, purchasers wanted access the founder cannot provide, or revenue depends on endless fresh unpaid content production. Repeat once before treating novelty purchases as retention.

#### 5. A high-specificity visitor or occasion concierge

**User/pain:** A visitor, host of out-of-town guests or special-occasion organizer has a deadline and several constraints, making a bad choice costly. “Find me dinner” is broad; “plan my parents' short visit around mobility, quiet conversation and two meal stops” is a concrete job.

**Offer/switch:** A reviewed, feasible small plan with transparent reasoning and backup options; where authorized, reservation assistance. Value would be accountable execution and current local knowledge, not generated prose. Google Ask Maps, guides, hotel concierge, friends, tour operators and general assistants are serious substitutes.

**Buyer/channel:** One-off planning fee paid by the organizer. Recruit through an actual local host/creator or permissioned traveler community; hotel referrals are not available merely because hotels exist. Occasional demand means repeat purchase may be low; referral and acquisition cost matter more than daily active use.

**Data/operations/advantage:** Small geographic coverage, but high verification burden and brittle reservation/hours facts. Different occasions are heterogeneous. Domain expertise and referral relationships can become advantages; an itinerary generator is easy to copy. Do not promise dietary/medical accessibility guarantees beyond verified venue information.

**Test/kill:** Offer five paid, tightly scoped planning engagements at a clearly stated experimental fee, and measure hours per plan, revisions, usage and referrals. Reject if free tools consistently suffice, if customers will not pay before extensive bespoke work, or if the required expertise is absent. This could be a viable service business without being a scalable app business; decide which the founder wants.

#### 6. A narrow recommender, retained only as a baseline experiment

**User/pain:** A repeat local diner genuinely wants one unfamiliar-but-approachable dish/restaurant at a predictable moment and dislikes searching. This is closest to current Nosh, but the technical ontology is not evidence the pain is valuable.

**Offer/switch:** Reliable weekly discovery for one real cohort, with human-verified local options and a clear reason to try. Test against the person's existing choice method, not against an artificially bad generic directory.

**Substitutes/buyer/channel:** Google, Yelp, Beli, DoorDash, Nudge, friends and habitual favorites. Consumer payment is unproven. Reels can recruit a test cohort only if viewers take action. Pure better-ranking claims, fewer cards or cross-cuisine terminology do not create a structural advantage.

**Data/operations:** No social density requirement, but thin feedback and local dish availability still make personalization hard. Broad catalog building before behavior validation is high risk. A manually curated shortlist can test the promise with almost no new infrastructure.

**Test/kill:** Ten to fifteen qualified diners receive several feasible choices over two weeks, with confirmed tried/liked outcomes and voluntary repeat requests. Test a paid next period. Stop if novelty wears off, users already get equivalent value from incumbents, or serving accurate suggestions takes too much founder labor. Keep as a learning/product hobby if useful personally; do not reframe it as a defensible business without evidence.

#### 7. Pause or stop product development

This is a valid strategic outcome, not failure to brainstorm. Existing code is a sunk cost and reusable engineering practice. If no reachable group has repeated costly pain, no one commits to a real pilot, or no promising pilot earns continuation/payment, avoid financing another ontology/data project with founder time. Preserve the MVP and research, pursue a more accessible problem, and revisit only when a concrete user/buyer/channel appears. The test is not whether people say the reel looks cool; it is whether a specific person changes behavior, accepts friction, pays or repeatedly returns.

### Gaps
- No pivot currently has demonstrated Nosh-specific demand or distribution. Ranking them confidently by market size would be invented precision.
- All suggested sample sizes and kill thresholds need to be agreed before pilots and interpreted cautiously; small pilots expose failure modes and commercial interest, not causal market-wide estimates.

## What shortlist follows from this evidence?

### Takeaway
Prioritize access to a reachable buyer and repeated outcome, not conceptual novelty. There is no unconditional winner from desk research. The shortlist below is conditional and explicitly an inference.

### Cited Findings
- Existing free tools already cover ordinary polling and shared availability ([Partiful](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525423-using-find-a-time-to-poll-guests-on-date-and-time), [Howbout](https://howbout.app/get-help/is-there-a-free-shared-calendar-app-for-friends/)); proactive local plans and creator content have advertised paid alternatives ([Nudge](https://www.nudgetext.com/plus), [Beli](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/beli/id1478375386)). This supports testing outcome differences and payment rather than assuming a missing feature is enough.

### Inferences
- **Best if a community buyer is accessible:** the closed-community lunch ritual. It concentrates distribution and timing, reduces venue scope and identifies a possible payer. Reject absent access or renewal.
- **Best if the founder wants to be a creator/operator:** one paid local experience or narrow creator-led club. It can sell a tangible result before software and may exploit the founder's interest in reels. Reject if the founder primarily wants a software business without content/event operations or lacks audience/host expertise.
- **Best low-cost consumer learning test:** private-group planning for existing pairs/groups, with saved intent as an input. Test the organizer's avoided labor and paid renewal. Do not market it as a novel social app.
- **Secondary/conditional:** specific visitor concierge if real expertise and referral channel exist. It has clearer episodic stakes but labor-heavy delivery and weak recurring use.
- **Weak standalone starting points:** generic saved-place activation, another cross-cuisine recommender, general food graph/semantic search, creator marketplace without creators, and nearby-stranger matching without concentrated density. Each makes a feature or supply investment before verifying an underserved customer and channel.
- Spend the next effort on problem interviews with artifacts: show the last abandoned save, last group planning thread, last failed outing and actual spending. Ask what happened, what alternative was used, who did the work and what it cost. Follow with one paid/behavioral pilot. A pricing question in an interview is not equivalent to a purchase.
- Infrastructure can stay within a modest experimentation budget by using manual verification and existing communication/event tools, but founder labor, acquisition, host time and refunds are costs even when server spending is near zero. Do not claim a business is sustainable merely because hosting is cheap.

### Gaps
- Founder preferences are consequential: software vs service, willingness to host or sell, existing reachable communities and appetite for operational responsibility. These should be clarified before committing to the conditional shortlist.

## How does the shortlist change when scalable product growth is the goal?

### Takeaway
Private-group planning has the cleanest potential consumer unit; closed-community rituals have a plausible concentrated distribution model; creator-led utility offers borrowed distribution only if creators actually adopt and retain it. None has established demand. Generic concierge and hosted experiences drop in priority as businesses because fulfillment remains tied to labor, though they can expose a repeatable job to automate. Saved-intent activation is an ingredient to test, not the presumptive winning pivot.

### Cited Findings
- Cobble already frames discovery around agreeing on what to do; Partiful already connects polling and RSVP; Howbout offers shared availability. These establish a serious group-planning baseline. [Cobble](https://www.trycobble.com/), [Partiful](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525423-using-find-a-time-to-poll-guests-on-date-and-time), [Howbout](https://howbout.app/get-help/is-there-a-free-shared-calendar-app-for-friends/)
- Donut has a free introduction product within Slack; Timeleft organizes real-world encounters; Beli and Thatch advertise creator monetization. These facts constrain workplace, social and creator-distribution claims. [Donut](https://www.donut.com/pricing/), [Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/faq/), [Beli](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/beli/id1478375386), [Thatch](https://thatch.notion.site/Become-a-Seller-bd37d343266141b78203137f25424852)

### Inferences

**Candidate A: a recurring private-group commitment product, starting with meals.** The recurring action is a known group agreeing and attending, not scrolling restaurant recommendations. An individual organizer can obtain value immediately without waiting for a citywide network. The guest invitation is a potential acquisition loop: a recipient can later organize another group. This is a hypothesis, not automatic viral growth. The first recruitment route is direct contact with 5–10 organizers the founder can actually identify, plus a small public opt-in recruitment post where permitted. If that pool cannot be named/reached, recruiting organizers is the first falsification exercise; do not pretend reel distribution solves it.

The test must include a serious incumbent alternative: offer the same group an easy Partiful/Cobble/group-chat flow for one comparable outing and a manually operated Nosh flow for another. Counterbalance order if possible, document organizer effort and completed plans, and do not claim statistical lift from a handful of groups. Nosh is only interesting if repeatedly carrying the plan from preferences to a deadline and explicit commitment removes work left over after the tools. If the only improvement is a prettier poll, stop.

The service-to-software path is a narrow state machine: group constraints → a small set of verified feasible options → acceptance deadline → scheduled plan → reminders and outcome. Reuse user-submitted options and a small venue pool to avoid an editorial empire. Manual pilot logs should identify recurring tasks that can be automated without reducing reliability. Proposed milestone before a larger build: several groups complete three cycles, at least some new organizers arise from invited guests, and operator time per completed plan falls materially as templates/reuse improve. Monetary model remains uncertain: a host premium tier or eventual transaction/partner model requires separate evidence and cannot be justified by assuming restaurants subsidize it. Reasons not to pursue: people do not actually want more outings, recipients refuse another interaction surface, free chat is enough, or coordination remains bespoke.

**Candidate B: self-service recurring lunch groups inside an existing community.** The recurring action is weekly opt-in and attendance; the buyer/distribution unit is a community organizer. A first route is personally approaching a small, named set of coworking/community/campus organizers and asking for one permissioned trial with their existing members. The founder's access is presently unknown; no sponsor after a bounded outreach effort is meaningful negative evidence. Software could later let organizers configure schedules, group sizes, venue rules and recurring attendance without the founder hosting. Community-by-community deployment may produce an efficient channel, but sales and setup costs could overwhelm a low-priced product.

Compare with a plain lunch Slack channel or Donut-style introductions plus the existing host. Measure incremental organizer time saved, recurrence, comfort and attendance. A group matching algorithm alone is not enough. To justify scaling, a second organizer outside the founder's own circle must run the program with minimal onboarding, venues must fit a simple repeatable template, and the program must continue without founder attendance. Reasons not to pursue: community owners will not recruit, do not pay, require bespoke events, or members only join because the founder actively hosts. This is potentially B2B2C infrastructure, not automatically a mass consumer network; keep that business choice explicit.

**Candidate C: a creator-distributed action layer, not another guide marketplace.** The recurring action is a follower using a trusted creator's local collection to execute an outing and return for the next relevant plan. Initial utility might be a creator-branded, current shortlist with concrete routes/choices and follow-through; no full app install should be required to establish value. The first route is outreach to a bounded list of small SF creators with an identifiable audience and existing restaurant content. Request one live trial placement in their existing channel, not a vague partnership promise. If creators will not distribute without large upfront payment, the low-budget acquisition premise fails.

The scalable path would be self-serve creator publishing and audience action tracking with reusable place data, with creators providing expertise and distribution. This adds a second customer: creators must gain revenue, reduced DM work or stronger audience outcomes beyond Thatch/Beli/Mapstr. Do not build a marketplace until supply and repeated reader usage are independently demonstrated. A live hosted event can test trust and appetite but is not evidence followers want software; separately test an unhosted artifact that delivers value without creator or founder escort. Require a creator to publish a second collection voluntarily and followers to return for a second use. Reasons not to pursue: the main value is the creator personally hosting, the platform creates no increment beyond a map link, creators demand exclusive audiences but will not promote, or each guide needs extensive manual upkeep. A creator product is easy to copy technologically; distribution relationships and utility to creators would have to become durable over time.

**Lower-priority scalable bets:** A general saved-intent reminder is readily testable but weak as a standalone paid product and crowded. A narrow recommender may grow gradually if repeat outcomes are unusually good, but building a world food graph before validation raises cost without improving channel access. A traveler concierge may monetize an urgent problem, yet episodic use and bespoke labor make it a poorer match to the clarified goals unless a very repeatable niche and acquisition channel emerges. It can be a discovery method rather than the eventual company.

**What could become an advantage, without inventing a moat:** an invitation loop that empirically creates new retained organizers; a repeatable community deployment with low sales/support costs; or creator partnerships that repeatedly bring engaged users because the product improves a creator's business. These are measurable mechanisms. “Our graph gets smarter,” “users will share,” and “incumbents do not care about small niches” are insufficient substitutes for evidence.

### Gaps
- No candidate yet has demonstrated retention, conversion, low-cost distribution or a viable paid model. Substantial company scale cannot be concluded from this desk research.
- These sources do not measure how much planning work remains after using competitors. That is the key empirical uncertainty for the group candidates.
- The founder's concrete reachable contacts and willingness to sell to organizers/creators remain unknown; this is a first research action, not a reason to assume distribution exists.
