# DoorDash recommendation architecture and implications for Nosh

## What do DoorDash's published systems actually do?

### Takeaway
DoorDash describes multiple, evolving recommendation systems: retrieve feasible candidates from a catalog, rank them with user and context signals, then apply surface-specific diversification or other rules. Its newer semantic profiles enrich that system; they do not replace its menus, merchants, transactions or operational facts. These are dated first-party engineering accounts, not a complete audit of every currently deployed DoorDash surface as of November 1, 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Homepage, October 5, 2022.** First-pass retrieval selects up to 1,200 stores; second-pass filtering/pre-ranking chooses up to 50 feed stores and ranks horizontal carousel contents; final ranking orders mixed stores/carousels vertically. The universal ranker targets conversion probability. Features include entity cuisine, price, popularity, quality and ratings; user tastes, dietary and affordability signals; view/click/order history, ratings and reorder behavior; and contextual ETA, distance, fees, promotions, day/week part, holidays and weather. Embeddings supplement numerical/categorical features. An upper-confidence-bound exploration mechanism seeks promising uncertain choices and addresses over-concentration on familiar/popular merchants. This is the documented 2022 final-ranking design, not proof all stages remain identical today. [Homepage recommendation with exploitation and exploration](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/homepage-recommendation-with-exploitation-and-exploration/)
- **Retail store page, December 12, 2023.** This is grocery/convenience/retail, not a claim about the restaurant home feed. Collections come from operators, rules or ML; retrieval chooses collections, an item ranker orders their contents, post-processing adjusts items, collection ranking follows, and final post-processing diversifies/deduplicates. The article describes down-ranking likely out-of-stock items. Click-focused ranking caused undesirable effects; positive examples received more weight when clicks led to add-to-cart and conversion. Maximal marginal relevance diversifies recommendations after ranking. Context includes geolocation and time; signals also include product price, categories, user dietary preference and price sensitivity. [Personalizing the DoorDash retail store page experience](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/personalizing-the-doordash-retail-store-page-experience/)
- **Search, November 19, 2024.** Search documents are items or stores. Knowledge-graph taxonomy metadata enriches indexed documents; queries are segmented and linked to dish, cuisine, meal and dietary concepts. Approximate-nearest-neighbor retrieval supplies candidate taxonomy labels to an LLM, constraining its outputs. The resulting linked concepts help retrieve real catalog items. The article contrasts strict dietary conditions with flexible flavor preferences and describes post-processing and human audits. Batch processing known queries is combined with methods that generalize to new queries, including lexical and embedding retrieval. Rankers consume the new signals. [How DoorDash leverages LLMs for better search retrieval](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/how-doordash-leverages-llms-for-better-search-retrieval/)
- **Profiles, July 29, 2025.** Consumer inputs include ordered items/restaurants, descriptions, prices, tags and ordering time. Merchant inputs include a year's item sales, menu categories/titles and ratings/reviews; item inputs include images, descriptions and customization options. SQL/scripts extract factual quantities and explicit tags; LLMs synthesize descriptive narratives. Consumer and merchant profile pipelines are described as running batch jobs; the post says item generation was planned for production by end-2025, so that sentence alone is not proof it shipped. Metadata-derived profiles can support new merchant/item cold starts. [Profile generation with LLMs](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/doordash-profile-generation-llms-understanding-consumers-merchants-and-items/)
- **Content embeddings, April 14, 2026.** Profiles become offline merchant/item embeddings, refreshed when source content changes. Inputs include menu items/descriptions/prices, order aggregates, ratings and merchant hours/location. Search embeds queries online, retrieves stores/items, and an experiment adds a fine-tuned reranker using query, item profiles and store profile. The article also describes generated homepage themes retrieving stores/dishes within delivery radius before existing store ranking. Semantic representations help sparse-history/new-item cases, but a single text-profile vector loses users' contextual and temporal distinctions. Offline evaluation includes LLM-judged similarity/relevance; reported online outcomes include conversions and order rates, not verified meal enjoyment. Generative semantic IDs are discussed as future direction, not established deployment. [Using LLMs to build content embeddings for search and recommendations](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/doordash-llms-to-build-content-embeddings-for-search-and-recommendations/)
- **Personalized grocery carousels, May 27, 2026.** Offline LLM calls consume task-specific consumer-memory subsets and generate structured titles and search intents, with abstention when evidence is insufficient. Generated intents are stored/indexed. Online serving retrieves actual catalog items through semantic and structured-taxonomy paths, merges/ranks results and assembles carousels. Deterministic quality gates check generated artifacts. This example deliberately separates expensive generation from fast request-time retrieval; it does not establish that DoorDash never uses online language-model ranking elsewhere. [Offline LLMs, online personalization](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/doordash-offline-llms-online-personalization-generating-carousels/)
- **Consumer memory, June 8, 2026.** DoorDash calls collaborative filtering and engagement models its personalization backbone, enriched by semantic memory. It distinguishes long-term memory, current-session context and explicit preferences; current activity can override history, while explicit constraints differ from inferred tastes. Long-term memory is computed daily/weekly, grounded in behavioral and catalog evidence. Versioned components carry provenance. Memories feed embeddings and a consumer-context graph; two-tower retrieval and multi-task rankers consume these alongside existing engagement signals. The authors emphasize that memory becomes valuable only when mapped reliably back to item-level catalog signals. [Building a unified consumer memory](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/doordash-unified-consumer-memory-for-personalization-at-scale/)

### Inferences
- An accurate plain-language synthesis is: **understand the request/person → find real eligible options → score them for this person in this context → adjust the final presentation → learn from responses.** There is no evidence here for “ask a general LLM for a dish and trust that somewhere nearby sells it.”
- A food ontology, user profile and embedding are complementary. The ontology defines concepts/relations; the profile represents evidence about a person or item; embeddings help retrieve related candidates; the ranker chooses among feasible options. None independently guarantees an appealing meal.
- Do not imply that all DoorDash models maximize enjoyment, that every recommendation is an LLM call, or that the 2022 numeric retrieval limits define its entire 2026 architecture. Each article describes a particular component/surface and development stage.

### Gaps
- Public articles do not reveal the complete current ranking formula, all production rollouts, every eligibility rule, or the exact relative weighting of business and consumer objectives.
- Reported company experiment results are not independently reproduced here and should not become expected Nosh uplift estimates.

## What is worth adapting for a dish-first Nosh that only locates restaurants?

### Takeaway
Copy the separation between semantic understanding and grounded retrieval. Nosh does not need delivery infrastructure, but it still needs evidence that a particular restaurant serves the dish before promising that match.

### Cited Findings
- The profile article builds on actual menu metadata, transactions, customization and reviews; the 2026 embeddings article explicitly refreshes representations as menu content changes. These are evidence-dependent enrichment systems. [Profiles](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/doordash-profile-generation-llms-understanding-consumers-merchants-and-items/), [Embeddings](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/doordash-llms-to-build-content-embeddings-for-search-and-recommendations/)
- Online retrieval remains grounded in a changing catalog even when personalized search intents are generated offline. [Offline/online carousels](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/doordash-offline-llms-online-personalization-generating-carousels/)

### Inferences
- Suggested Nosh architecture, intentionally much smaller: (1) a modest dish vocabulary and honest descriptive attributes; (2) explicit likes, current craving and confirmed enjoyment; (3) local, sourced dish-to-restaurant/menu mappings; (4) hard feasibility checks for location, hours and budget where evidence exists; (5) a deterministic score balancing taste match, evidence quality and bounded novelty; (6) a short explanation and Maps handoff; (7) a confirmation prompt that distinguishes tried from liked.
- Treat a restaurant's cuisine label as a candidate-search hint, not proof it serves a particular dish. A map/place provider supplies location identity; a menu source supplies dish evidence. Removing delivery removes ordering/payment/dispatch work, but not this missing join.
- Start with explicit preferences and a manually verified local catalog; Nosh will not initially possess DoorDash's order volume, exposure logs, menu feeds, review signals or learned representations. User-entered favorites and a small number of confirmed outcomes can test value before copying the industrial stack.
- Use LLMs, if later authorized, to assist offline normalization and drafting against cited menus; keep unknown attributes unknown. Recheck fast-changing feasibility at serving time. Do not turn an LLM's typical-recipe knowledge into a claim about the restaurant's actual version.
- Evaluate three distinct qualities: **relevance** (does this fit the stated preference?), **grounding** (can the named restaurant actually provide it?), and **outcome** (did the user try and enjoy it?). Strong retrieval scores cannot compensate for weak grounding, and a Yes/click cannot establish enjoyment.
- A particularly useful competitive distinction could be helping users take a manageable step into unfamiliar dishes while measuring enjoyment, rather than reproducing an e-commerce feed. That is a product hypothesis to validate, not a defensibility claim supported by DoorDash's articles.

### Gaps
- These engineering articles provide no permission or API for Nosh to access DoorDash's menu catalog or private profiles. Data acquisition and licensing remain separate research questions.
- They do not prove a particular cross-cuisine transfer rule works for Nosh's cohort, or that users prefer dish-first to restaurant-first discovery.
