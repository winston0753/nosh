# Iran war and energy transmission

## Has the energy and geopolitical landscape changed?

### Takeaway
Physical supply has improved materially from the initial March disruption, but neither diplomatic settlement nor normalization is established as of November 2, 2026. Today's oil selloff reflects improving expectations, not proof that the inflation and growth damage has reversed.

### Cited Findings
- On November 2 at 13:57 GMT (05:57 Pacific Standard Time), Reuters reported Brent futures at $106.65/bbl, down 6.0%, and WTI at $97.75, down 6.1%. Intraday lows were $105.86/$97.12. Brent had ended October at a six-month high. These are a timestamped intraday snapshot, not settlement or physical spot. Do not use changing quote widgets embedded around this story as the article's prices. [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/oil-slips-more-than-1-as-talks-continue-on-ending-iran-war-ce785fd8dd8cf62d)
- A separate November 2 Reuters report says negotiations continued through intermediaries after Doha talks ended without a breakthrough. Iranian sources believed another US attack more likely than agreement; that is attributed opinion, not an established probability. [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://sa.marketscreener.com/news/iran-says-contacts-over-ending-war-continue-as-talks-end-with-no-breakthrough-ce785fd8de8af12c)
- Iran's proposal to reopen Hormuz within two days was conditional on US steps, including ending the blockade; it was not an announcement that unrestricted transit had resumed. October saw the busiest month of reported commercial shipping attacks since March. [AP November 2](https://apnews.com/article/b4519ef86bafe2672e0855d7a48ce309)
- March 9 Reuters intraday highs were $119.50 Brent and $119.48 WTI futures, following US-Israel attacks beginning February 28. At the November 2 snapshot those contracts' prices were approximately 10.8% and 18.2% below those March intraday highs (own calculation). This is not a same-contract holding-period return: front-month contracts roll. [Reuters March 9 via ThePrint](https://theprint.in/world/oil-prices-hit-highest-since-2022-at-more-than-119-a-barrel-on-iran-war/2874056/)
- AP October 30 described nearly $120 in March as the war-period high for the most actively traded Brent futures contract. This independently supports the historical benchmark, while not determining subsequent prices after publication. [AP October 30](https://apnews.com/article/852851049c1ede6811516fe92700dcbe)
- The IEA March 12 report described Hormuz flows falling from roughly 20m b/d to a trickle and at least 10m b/d of Gulf production cuts. Treat this as early-war history, not current shipping conditions. [IEA March](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-march-2026)

### Inferences
- Lower oil on constructive talks can help tech through inflation expectations, bond yields, consumption and risk appetite. But a one-day move can reverse without verified safe shipping, repaired infrastructure and sustained restocking. The coexistence of diplomacy and escalation warnings means event risk remains two-sided, rather than establishing either imminent peace or inevitable renewed full-scale war.
- The midterms are relevant partly as an event-risk boundary for military decisions; that does not establish an economic law that oil or stocks must reverse after voting.

### Gaps
- No independently verified post-election outcome exists at this November 2 cutoff. No signed final settlement or unrestricted Hormuz reopening was verified.
- Exact latest US-close oil settlements were not established; the report must use the explicit intraday timestamp or update quotes before publication.

## Are oil prices only a risk premium, or is physical scarcity persisting?

### Takeaway
Supply recovery is real, but constrained transport, depleted inventory and refined-fuel scarcity make this more than a headline premium. Futures, physical crude and retail fuels must not be conflated.

### Cited Findings
- IEA October 14 (September estimates): Gulf crude exports recovered to 13.9m b/d, over 75% of prewar levels; Hormuz oil flows rose to 10.9m b/d, while bypass exports reached 5.6m b/d. Refined-product exports remained 1.9m b/d against 4.7m prewar. Observed global stocks were 509m barrels below end-February. September on-land inventories fell 113m barrels while oil afloat rose 103m, leaving a 10m net draw. Physical North Sea Dated was around $134/bbl at writing versus Brent futures around $107. It forecast 2026 demand contracting 2.6m b/d and deficits persisting through year-end despite demand weakness. These are estimates/forecasts, not final full-year results. [IEA October](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-october-2026)
- EIA October 6, forecast completed October 1: September Brent spot averaged $114, versus $91 in August; a September 15 daily price reached $131 after East-West pipeline attacks. Estimated production shut-ins eased to 4.8m b/d from August's 5.8m and May's 10.9m peak. EIA projected 4Q Brent spot $105/bbl, majority of regional output returning by end-2Q27, and $74 average Brent in 4Q27. Those conditional forecasts assume alternative export routes, recovering supply and inventory rebuilding; they are not observed forward prices. EIA estimated 3Q global inventories drawing 1.9m b/d, and forecast 4Q draws of 0.7m b/d. [EIA October global oil](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/global_oil.php)

### Inferences
- Current conditions differ from March: restored flows reduce the acute shortfall, but smaller inventory buffers make a further disruption potentially costly. Neither 'Hormuz is completely closed today' nor 'the crisis is over because oil is off its highs' is supported.
- Physical/futures price divergence indicates nearby delivery and logistics stress. Do not calculate one continuous drawdown using March futures highs, September EIA spot, October IEA Dated and November futures: these are different instruments and assessment dates.
- EIA and IEA do not use identical balances or forecast vintages. Report each series with its own agency and period; do not average them or pretend a small difference is a contradiction. EIA October release was completed before IEA October release.

### Gaps
- Full October physical-flow estimates and November observed inventory balances are not available in the cited monthly reports. The November EIA STEO was scheduled for November 10, after this cutoff.
- Industry statements of nearly 1 billion barrels drained, cited in November 2 Reuters reporting, should not replace IEA's 509m observed-stock decline: definitions, coverage and dates differ and were not reconciled. Omit that industry estimate from headline quantitative conclusions.

## Does energy imply another tech downturn, or a changed investment regime?

### Takeaway
Persistent energy scarcity supports a stagflation-risk interpretation of the tech correction, but it does not prove recession or an AI earnings collapse. Durable energy normalization would weaken that bearish thesis; headline diplomacy alone has not done so.

### Cited Findings
- EIA October petroleum report: September US gasoline averaged $4.35/gal and diesel $6.29/gal. East Coast distillate inventories were 32% below the 2021–25 seasonal average. It projected elevated diesel prices and inventories below normal through 2027, and noted wholesale price declines need not immediately reach retail buyers. These are product-specific prices, not an all-grades monthly series. [EIA petroleum products](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/petro_prod.php)
- BLS October 21 publication of September data: final-demand PPI rose 6.3% year on year; energy goods prices rose 35.4%, diesel 115.2%, and transportation/warehousing services 16.0%. These are producer prices and cannot be quoted as consumer inflation. BLS notes revision risk. [BLS September PPI article](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/producer-price-index-up-6-3-percent-in-september-largest-12-month-increase-since-december-2022.htm)
- BIS paper published October 19 models inflation transmission using data only through May 2026. Its finding of a smaller, shorter shock than 2021–24 and stable long-run inflation expectations is historical/model evidence; it does not incorporate September infrastructure attacks or October prices. Do not present its 1.5–2 percentage-point cumulative inflation estimate as a live November forecast. [BIS paper](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1384-inflation-after-iran-war)
- EIA October electricity outlook anticipates 2026 wholesale electricity $52/MWh across 11 hubs, up 11%, versus a projected $49 in 2027. It identifies gas prices and weather as important influences and distinguishes wholesale energy from retail grid/distribution/capacity costs. [EIA electricity](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/electricity.php)

### Inferences
- Bearish transmission: fuel and transport prices squeeze household real spending and business margins; sustained inflation restricts room for Fed easing; higher required yields can compress growth-stock multiples. These are plausible channels rather than measured attribution of the Nasdaq correction.
- Bullish countercase: lasting shipping restoration and rising stocks reduce oil prices and uncertainty, improving purchasing power and potential financial conditions before lagging inflation data improve. Oil falling because recession destroys demand would be a much weaker signal than oil falling because supply returns.
- Tech is heterogeneous: software services have limited direct petroleum input, while hardware logistics, global demand and AI infrastructure can face different exposures. US data-center electricity costs do not move one-for-one with Brent; local gas, grid and capacity constraints matter. Avoid calling expensive crude a direct equivalent increase in AI power expense.
- Monitor a joint confirmation set: multiweek export/transit recovery; fewer tanker/pipeline attacks; on-land inventory stabilization rather than merely more oil on water; falling physical premiums, diesel cracks and freight/insurance costs; lower broad inflation expectations alongside stable employment/credit. Conversely, renewed infrastructure damage, rising delivered fuel prices and credit deterioration strengthen the warning interpretation.
- Market direction cannot be determined from oil in isolation: relative tech performance may improve in a weak economy if cash-rich firms maintain earnings while cyclicals deteriorate. Higher energy-company earnings also do not indicate stronger aggregate real growth.

### Gaps
- No quantitative causal estimate of how much of the latest technology drawdown was caused by Iran, crude, inflation or changing AI expectations was established. Such decomposition requires a specified window, returns series and identification strategy.
- No reliable single oil-price threshold establishes recession. Duration, real price, growth backdrop, monetary reaction and supply-versus-demand cause all matter.
